## Creating the Buddha with a stone-like texture

The most prominent part of this tattoo is the Buddha's face, which occupies a large portion of the upper arm.

Instead of treating the surface as completely smooth, subtle cracks and transitions between light and shadow were incorporated into the face to suggest the appearance of a stone sculpture.

The cracks are concentrated around selected areas of the forehead and cheeks, while the eyes, nose and lips retain enough visual space to keep the face from becoming overly busy.

In this approach, the cracks are not intended to become the main subject. They work as supporting texture, helping the viewer read the Buddha as a figure rendered with the appearance of aged stone.

### Light and shadow create depth in the face

Adding cracks alone is not enough to make a tattoo feel like a stone sculpture.

The sense of volume comes largely from the balance between light and shadow. Areas such as the forehead, bridge of the nose, cheekbones and lips are separated from the deeper shadows along the sides of the face.

This allows the Buddha's face to remain the first element the eye recognizes from a distance. When viewed more closely, the cracks and smaller stone-texture details begin to appear.

## The mudra, Om symbol and lotus in the composition

Below the Buddha's face are a hand forming a **mudra**, the **Om (ॐ)** symbol and a lotus flower.

These elements help the composition transition naturally from the large facial area toward the lower section of the arm. They also create a visual and symbolic connection throughout the tattoo.

### The hand gesture — a Buddhist mudra

The symbolic hand positions commonly seen in Buddhist imagery are known as **mudras**.

In simple terms, mudras are specific hand gestures used symbolically in Buddhist art and spiritual practice. Different mudras can carry different meanings depending on the position of the hands and fingers, as well as the context in which they appear.

In this tattoo, the hand gesture visually connects the Buddha's face above with the Om symbol and lotus below.

From a compositional perspective, it also creates a gradual transition from the large scale of the face to the smaller elements toward the lower part of the design.

## What does the Om symbol (ॐ) mean?

**Om**, commonly represented by the symbol **ॐ**, is a sacred syllable and symbol originating from Indian religious and philosophical traditions.

It is widely associated with Hindu traditions and also appears in some Buddhist traditions, particularly within mantras. A familiar example is the opening sound of **Om Mani Padme Hum**, a well-known mantra in Tibetan Buddhism.

Because Om appears across different traditions and contexts, it would be misleading to treat it as a word with only one simple English definition.

More broadly, Om is often associated with spiritual awareness, meditation, concentration and turning one's attention inward.

Within this tattoo, the Om symbol supports the contemplative character of the overall Buddha composition rather than functioning only as a decorative element.

## The lotus completes the lower part of the tattoo

The lotus flower sits at the bottom of the composition.

In Buddhist art, the lotus is a familiar symbol often associated with purity and the idea of rising from imperfect conditions while remaining untainted.

Visually, the lotus also provides a natural ending to the tattoo.

The Buddha's face occupies the largest area at the top, followed by the mudra and Om symbol, before the composition gradually narrows toward the lotus.

Most of the tattoo uses Black & Grey tones, while the lotus carries a subtle touch of color. This creates a visual accent at the bottom without competing with the Buddha's face, which remains the main focus of the piece.

## A composition designed around the male upper arm

The upper arm is a curved surface, so a design that looks balanced on a flat image does not always behave the same way when placed on the body.

In this tattoo, the Buddha's face receives the largest amount of space and sits across the main visual area of the upper arm.

Darker areas running along the sides help frame the face while connecting the upper section of the tattoo with the details below.

The mudra, Om symbol and lotus become progressively smaller as the design moves down the arm. This hierarchy allows the viewer to see the Buddha's face first, then naturally follow the composition downward.

For this reason, with detailed designs like this one, Lan Pink usually considers the actual tattoo placement and the proportions of the client's arm before finalizing the composition rather than simply enlarging or reducing an existing reference image.
