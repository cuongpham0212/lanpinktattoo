# Artistic Fish Forearm Tattoo in Red and Black

This **fish forearm tattoo** was created for an international male client who has returned to Lan Pink Tattoo for several tattoo sessions.

For this piece, the fish was designed to follow the natural length and shape of the forearm rather than simply placing a standalone image on the skin.

Red and black are the main colors. The body of the fish creates the primary focal point, while the fins, tail and surrounding lines help carry the composition vertically along the arm.

Open areas of skin are also left between the lines and color sections. This gives the tattoo more breathing room and prevents the entire forearm from becoming one heavy block of color.

The result is a fish tattoo with a clear subject, visible movement and a composition that works naturally with the shape of the forearm.

## What stands out about this fish forearm tattoo?

One of the most noticeable features is the **vertical composition**.

Instead of positioning the fish horizontally, the design uses the length of the forearm as part of the composition.

The head creates a strong focal point in the upper section. The body then curves naturally downward, while the tail continues the visual movement toward the lower forearm.

This helps the tattoo feel connected to the arm rather than looking like an image that was simply placed on top of it.

When viewed as a whole, the eye naturally follows the fish from its head through the body and down toward the tail.

For a longer tattoo on the forearm, this kind of visual flow can make a significant difference to the overall composition.

## Why does a fish work well as a forearm tattoo?

A fish is a versatile subject for a long, narrow area such as the forearm.

Its body can curve and follow the shape of the arm, while the fins and tail can be adjusted to create movement and fill space without requiring a rigid square or circular composition.

In this piece, the body of the fish acts as the main visual axis.

The head creates the focal point, while the tail extends the design further down the arm. Smaller lines and details around the fish help connect the different areas of the composition.

This is something worth considering when choosing a **fish tattoo for the forearm**.

It is not only about finding a fish design you like. The shape and direction of that design also need to work with the part of the body where it will be tattooed.

## How do the red and black colors affect the design?

Red and black create the main contrast in this tattoo.

The red is especially noticeable around the head and body of the fish, helping the main subject stand out against the skin.

Darker areas provide contrast and help define the structure of the fish, while the surrounding linework keeps the design from becoming a single solid block of color.

Not every part of the forearm is completely filled.

Leaving areas of natural skin between the darker sections gives the composition more space and makes the main subject easier to read.

For someone who wants a color tattoo without using a large number of different colors, a red-and-black palette can be an interesting direction to explore.

## Why are open spaces important in a tattoo like this?

The uninked areas are also part of the composition.

If every section of a long forearm tattoo has the same visual weight, the design can easily begin to feel too dense.

In this piece, there is a balance between:

- linework;
- red color;
- darker areas;
- and natural skin.

These changes in density give the eye places to rest.

They also help separate the different parts of the fish so that the head, body, fins and tail do not merge into one heavy shape when viewed from a distance.

Open skin does not necessarily mean the design is unfinished.

Used intentionally, it can help make a tattoo feel lighter and improve the overall visual balance.

## How are linework and color combined in this tattoo?

The tattoo does not rely on color alone to define the fish.

Linework is used to establish the head, body, scales, fins and tail. Red and darker areas are then added to create contrast and depth.

This creates two different viewing experiences.

From farther away, the overall shape of the fish remains recognizable.

When viewed more closely, smaller details in the body and fins become more visible.

For a relatively long forearm tattoo, this is useful because the overall composition should still be readable before every small detail is noticed.

Trying to place too many tiny details across the entire design can make a tattoo feel visually crowded.

## What size works best for a fish forearm tattoo?

There is no single ideal size for everyone.

The size should depend on the length and shape of the forearm, the amount of detail in the design and how visible the client wants the tattoo to be.

If the design includes details in the head, scales, fins and tail, it needs enough space for those elements to remain separated.

Making a detailed design too small can force individual elements too close together.

For a smaller fish tattoo, simplifying the design may be a better approach.

A useful principle is:

**Choose a size that supports the design instead of forcing a detailed design into a size that is too small for it.**

This is especially relevant for fish tattoos because the body, scales, fins and tail can contain many individual details.

## Is a forearm tattoo highly visible?

The forearm is one of the more visible tattoo placements in everyday life.

With short sleeves, a tattoo in this area can become a noticeable part of your overall appearance.

At the same time, it can often be covered with long sleeves when necessary.

A longer design like this one creates a stronger visual presence than a small tattoo, especially when the arm is viewed from the side.

Before choosing the final size, it is worth considering how visible you want the tattoo to be in your everyday life.

For people who want the tattoo to become a clear visual feature of the arm, a vertical forearm composition can work particularly well.

## Can I use this tattoo as a reference for my own design?

Yes.

This tattoo can be used as a **reference for composition, color or placement** without copying the entire design.

A fish tattoo can change significantly by adjusting:

- the direction of the fish;
- the curve of the body;
- the shape and size of the fins;
- the tail;
- the proportion of the fish to the forearm;
- the amount of detail;
- the use of red and black;
- and the surrounding elements.

You might like the vertical composition but prefer a different fish.

You might like the red-and-black palette but want a simpler design.

Or you may want to keep the sense of movement while changing the size and details to better fit your own arm.

Using a tattoo as inspiration rather than copying it exactly also gives more room to adapt the design to your body.
